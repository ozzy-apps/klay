# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Plan Layout: face loops**: on a symmetric character the head is built finer than the body (0.6 of the quad) and the face is laid out as nested loops, as in a hand-made base mesh: a mask loop round both eyes from above the brows to the top of the nose, four rings round each eye (placed by the face's proportions), a loop round the nose and nostrils, and three rings round the mouth from under the nose to the chin; each a rectangle of the head's grid ringed in and capped, with poles only at its corners, exactly mirrored. If the eyes do not fit, the face is left as before. On a stylized T-pose base mesh at 10k quads: 0.8% badly shaped quads (0.5% before), no folded quads.

## 0.50.0 — 2026-11-08

- Hold **Shift** to snap the Transpose action line or constrain **Move** to one axis. In Gizmo 3D, Shift locks the free-move ring to one of the Gizmo’s axes.
- The viewport floor grid follows the lowest visible point across SubTools, including live Move, Scale and Rotate previews.
- Sculpting, masking, remeshing and other symmetry-aware edits use the active SubTool’s center. Merge Down uses the combined bounds of the merged geometry, including disconnected parts.
- **ZRemesher Follow Shape** (0–100, default 60): edge loops follow the way the surface bends. Each sample's bending (the second fundamental form, fitted to how the normal turns over two rings of neighbours and smoothed to about quad size) gives the direction it bends most; where the two bends clearly differ, the direction field is pulled toward it, by the slider, by how lopsided the bend is (0 on a ball or a plane, 1 on a cylinder or a saddle) and by how much it shows over one quad. Guides, polygroup borders and edge lines still win where they reach. Measured: mean misalignment of the loops on a torus 7.8° → 3.9°, on a vase 7.2° → 4.0°; a ball is unchanged.
- **Detect Edges** is on by default, so hard-surface parts get loops on their sharp edges without a click. Short scraps and doubled lines among the sharp edges (the noise a voxel remesh leaves along a ridge) are dropped before they steer the loops. With Plan Layout off: a voxel-remeshed bust 20.2% → 18.0% badly shaped quads (folded 329 → 305); a fused, armoured alien that failed with edges shared by more than two polygons now remeshes; a hard-surface pod 13.9% → 13.3% badly shaped, but folded quads 200 → 241.
- Verification: 464 release tests pass (15 ignored). The ZRemesher regression set (ball, cube, torus, vase, rook, blob, a bust with and without guide loops) is unchanged with Plan Layout on; a stylized T-pose base mesh comes out as an exact mirror image (no vertex over 0.02 quad from its mirror).
- **ZRemesher Plan Layout** (Geometry › ZRemesher, experimental, off) plans the whole quad layout before placing quads, in the manner of QuadWild: a cross field on a refined copy of the model, lines traced from its poles, patches, whole quad counts that neighbouring patches agree on, and regular fills (grids, centre splits for 3–8 sides, trapezoids, bands between loops), then relax and projection. Clean forms get long loops and few poles (a ball 8, a torus none, a cube 8). Closed ZRemesherGuide loops become edge loops round eyes, mouths and sockets (the inside a cap, four loops leaving). A second skin inside AI and solidified meshes is dropped, small handles are closed or cut round their short ring, big patches with a concave corner get a line from that corner, points are snapped to their own patch (no spikes) and folded quads untangled, and every run gives the same result. It falls back to the usual remesh when it cannot plan; the notice says which happened. On a detailed AI-generated bust: 7.1% badly shaped quads and 431 poles (3.8%).
- **Plan Layout builds bodies like a box modeller**: on a model with arms and legs, rings are laid a quad apart from the top of the head (distance along the surface) and joined row to row. The head is a quad sphere on a cylinder neck; the trunk, arms, legs and fingers are cylinders with one count each, bridged by strips where they branch (armpits, crotch, hands) so the edge loops match at every join; fingers run to their tips and hands and feet end in grid caps. Count changes are diamonds (one 3-pole and one 5-pole each) gathered on the back or inside of a limb. Breasts, eye sockets and the mouth are rectangle insets: concentric rings inside a rectangle of the grid, with four poles at its corners and none on the rings. Symmetric models (or with Symmetry on) come out as exact mirror images with a centre line down the front and back, kept through the final relax. Where a model loops back on itself (straps, fused collars) the rings that meet are bridged. On a stylized T-pose base mesh (250k triangles to 11k quads in about 7 s): 270 poles (2.5%), 0.5% badly shaped quads and no folded quads (the usual remesh: 6.7% poles); a fused, armoured alien builds at about 6% badly shaped quads (18% planned).

## 0.49.0 — 2026-11-05

- **Anti-aliasing**: the canvas is drawn with 4× multisampling. Zoomed out, a dense mesh puts many triangles in each pixel, which gave stair-stepped silhouettes and cut edges and a noisy fringe where the shading changes. Points for dense meshes get the same four samples: they land in a grid twice as fine, and each sample of the resolve takes its own point, so point-drawn outlines are smooth too. `KLAY_MSAA=1` turns it off (A/B, slow GPUs). GPUs without 4× multisampling for the window format draw as before.
- Cost at 15.4M vertices with vsync off: framed 3.0 → 4.1 ms, zoomed out 3.1 → 3.2 ms.
- Verification: 447 release tests. Every GPU self-test passes with it on, with no GPU validation errors. `--selftest-splat` at 15.4M: no empty model pixels, colour within 0.5/255 of triangles. A two-Clip-Line sphere at 8.6M seen at three distances: smooth outlines and cut edges where 1× stepped.

## 0.48.1 — 2026-11-05

- Fixed fuzzy cut edges, dotted seams and specks on dense meshes, most visible after Clip Line on 15M+ vertices. Chunks dense enough to draw as points now have to face the camera with every triangle by a clear margin (their normal cone, the angle to the camera and their size within 70°). Silhouettes and the folded surface a clip leaves on its cut are drawn as triangles. Points dropped vertices whose normals faced away; inside such chunks no triangle does, so every vertex is drawn and no holes open.
- Cost: at 15.4M vertices, framed, chunks drawn as points went from about 1,530 to about 730, and the frame from 1.7 to 2.5 ms with vsync off.
- Verification: 447 release tests. `--selftest-splat` and `--selftest-cull` at 15.4M pass: no empty model pixels, colour within 0.7/255 of triangles. A two-Clip-Line sphere at 8.6M, from five angles at full resolution, renders the same as with triangles only.

## 0.48.0 — 2026-11-05

- **Wrap** for alphas and textures (Alpha and Texture controls, 0–100, default 100; ZBrush: Wrap Mode). Stamps used to lie flat on the plane under the brush, so where a dab reached a rim, a box edge or the side of a thin part, every point down the turning surface read the same pixel and the alpha or texture streaked. The stamp distance now follows the surface: the arc of the circle through the dab centre and the point (exact on spheres and across cylinders), never below the plane distance plus 0.35 × height, so a box's side face keeps going. The stamp lies on a plane fitted to the surface as it was before the stroke, and the stretch eases in over the inner fifth of the radius, so the stroke's own relief does not cross-hatch it. Applies to sculpting and painting.
- Brush presets save Wrap (preset format 2; format 1 presets load with Wrap at 100). The brush-asset settings file stores it too.
- Verification: 447 release tests (new: the arc-length unwrap on a sphere, no fold past 90°, little movement from relief near the centre, a box side face moving at once, Wrap 0 equal to the flat projection, Wrap saved in a brush preset). Visual A/B on a 345k cube, with a striped alpha sculpt and a dotted texture paint along a face next to an edge: Wrap 0 streaks down the side face; Wrap 100 carries the pattern round it.

## 0.40.0 — 2026-11-03

- **ZModeler drags are live on dense meshes.** A drag used to turn the whole polygon view back into triangles and rebuild and re-upload the subtool at every cursor step (some 200 ms a step on a 200k-polygon ZRemesher result, so ZModeler only served modelling-sized meshes). Now an action that only moves points (Move, Scale, Transpose, point and edge Move, point Slide) says where they go, and one that makes polygons (QMesh, Extrude, Inset, Bevel, Inflate, Insert, Slide, Extrude edge, point Bevel) is built once, on the first step, after which its points move linearly with the drag's amount (every one is a `lerp` in it, so a second sample at another amount gives every later step for nothing); either way the vertices move in place and only their chunks go to the GPU, as a brush stroke's do. On release the triangles are remade only if a quad now wants its other diagonal. Measured on a 200 934-polygon sphere (release build, CPU side, `cargo test --release zmodeler_drag_steps -- --ignored --nocapture`): a Move step 200–300 ms → 0.4 ms, a QMesh step 110–170 ms → 93 ms for the first step and 0.4 ms after, an Insert step 340–400 ms → 0.5 ms (its build happens at the press).
- **ZModeler: the last polygon and edge actions.** Polygons: **Spin Edges** (the points round the polygons turn one slot, so a polygon turns within its neighbours; a mirror image turns the other way), **Align** (the points flattened onto the clicked polygon's plane), **Insert Point** (a point where the click is, joined to the corners: a triangle per edge), **Unweld** (the polygons come apart from their neighbours on points of their own), **Equalize** (each polygon made regular, equal edges and angles about its middle, all of them at their mean area) and **Transpose** (click to put Gizmo 3D on the polygons, then drag its arrows, boxes, rings and squares; the gizmo stays on them, carried along, until the topology changes). Edges: **Align** (the loop through the edge straightened: an open run onto the line through its ends, a closed loop onto its own plane), **Insert Point** (a point on the edge where the click is), **Unweld** (the mesh cut along the edge or its loop, the points duplicated so the sides come apart; a cut that ends inside the mesh leaves a slit) and **Transpose** (the edge or loop with the gizmo). EdgeLoop widens Unweld and Transpose to the loop. Creases follow Insert Point and Unweld.
- Verification: 415 tests, 13 ignored (new: every drag action that makes polygons is linear in its amount, so two samples predict a third within 1e-5; on a cube, Spin Edges turns a polygon's corners one slot and back, and moves exactly the two border loops of a ring; Align flattens a lifted corner, a pushed-out loop point and an open run's middle point; Insert Point makes four triangles in a polygon and two five-sided polygons on an edge with the crease split in two; Unweld floats a polygon on four points of its own and cuts the cube in two along a loop with creases on both sides, one edge alone leaving its two polygons apart; Equalize squares a skewed quad at its area and leaves squares alone; Transpose maps points and their mirror images; and through the input path: a five-step QMesh drag rebuilds once and lands where a one-step drag lands, a Move drag never rebuilds, each new click action lands with its polygon and point counts, an edge Unweld with EdgeLoop splits the cube into 34 points, and a Transpose click aims the gizmo without an undo step while a drag on its X arrow moves the four points along it, carries the gizmo and undoes). `--selftest-zmodeler` passes on the GPU (its GPU-copy checks now run a phase later, since a drag's chunks go up with the next frame, as a stroke's do).

## 0.33.0 — 2026-11-03

Its centre runs on the surface, so the root sits half inside the model; where the stroke runs off the model it carries on at the depth where it left, so a tube can hang free. Rings are as far apart as a side is wide, carried along the curve without twisting, with round caps at both ends. X symmetry adds the mirror tube in the same polygroup.
- The curve stays live: drag it to bend the tube (pulled points snap to the model under them, or stay in the air off it); changing Draw Size, Tube taper or Tube sides (Stroke › Curve) rebuilds it. Esc or any other edit lets it go. One undo step per tube and per bend; undo keeps only the tube, not a copy of the model.
- Like ZBrush's insert brushes it needs a subtool without subdivision levels, and says so otherwise. The tube is part of the mesh: it saves, exports, divides and can be Wedged with the rest.
- Catalog: 34 tools, new Curve filter. The tube is swapped in place, on the CPU (cut and extend the model's arrays and tables, never rebuild them) and on the GPU (buffers get room once, then only the tube's slice is written): a bend re-builds in 5–6 ms on a 966k-vertex model, against 78 ms rebuilding the model.
- Verification: 155 release tests (tube shape, closed and outward winding, no twist, append/cut/swap identical to a full rebuild, input path with bend/resize/undo/redo/project round trip/refusal); new --selftest-tube (nine tubes, a bend, GPU readback exact, undo/redo of all of them) at 4k and 966k vertices, plus every earlier native self-test.

## 0.5.0 — 2026-10-22

- Curve strokes (ZBrush Stroke › Curve): the stroke is drawn as a path and, on release, smoothed, laid onto the surface and applied along it. Zipper, Stitch and Rake start in Curve mode and are laid out in the curve's own frame, so a shaky hand gives even, square teeth; other dab brushes step evenly along it. The curve stays as a dashed line and can be dragged to bend it, re-applying the brush along the new shape; Esc lets it go. Freehand / Curve per brush on the left shelf stroke thumbnail and in Stroke › Curve, with Curve smooth and Curve step.
- Pen pressure on X11 (XInput2, any pen with an "Abs Pressure" axis, e.g. XP-Pen, Wacom, Huion): Draw Size and Z / RGB Intensity follow the pen, linear like ZBrush's default curves, with Size and Z Intensity toggles and a live readout in Brush › Tablet pressure. Drag brushes and the detail reliefs keep their size; a mouse strokes at full strength.
- Merged with the polygroup, primitive, Wedge and PolyF work from main (0.4.0 was built on it).
- Verification: 148 release tests; --selftest-curve (a shaky stroke's curve lies within 0.24 % of Draw Size of a steady stroke's in the middle; its Zipper strays 6–8 % from the steady line's, against 46 % freehand; bend, GPU readback, undo/redo exact) and --selftest-pressure, plus every earlier native self-test, on Xvfb/llvmpipe.
